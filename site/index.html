    The part nobody owns

    A deduction can have a tax deadline.

    When a brand settles a scheme claim with a GST credit note, it gets back the GST already paid on the discount. That credit note must go out by 30 November after the financial year ends (or the annual return date, if earlier). For FY 2025-26 supplies, that's 30 November 2026.

    Many brands settle schemes with a plain financial credit note instead and keep the GST cost. From 1 Nov 2026 the GST route gets easier, so more claims will qualify. An undecided deduction quietly loses that choice after the deadline.

    The brand's GST gain is the distributor's input-credit reversal. That's why the two sides negotiate which kind of credit note gets issued.

    The thinking

    Controls first, failure planned for

    What this costs
    • Fewer auto-matches at launch: strict matching rejects fuzzy ones.
    • More manual review: anything above ₹10,000 needs a second person.
    • A slower first close: Sales must enter scheme dates and budgets before rules can accept a claim.

    What I got wrong: v2 was a wall of text, so I rebuilt it as something you can run, because a spec nobody executes isn't a spec.

    How I'd work from week one

    1. Evidence first. Every fact dated and sourced.
    2. One owner per gap. One decision, one deadline.
    3. Testable rules. 14 rules, 27 tests, "must block" cases included.
    4. Controls built in. Maker-checker, no auto write-offs. Zero SOX findings at HDFC.

    Where it could fail, and the fix

    1. Distributors pay the old account. Keep it open a quarter; Sales gets a weekly list.
    2. Schemes change mid-quarter. No dates and budget from Sales, no valid scheme.
    3. Sales overrides the credit flag. It never blocks an order; overrides are logged by name.
