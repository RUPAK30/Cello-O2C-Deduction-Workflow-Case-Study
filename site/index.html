Business analysis case study · Order-to-cash · Rupak Ghanpathi

Every rupee needs an owner.

In Indian distribution, a short payment is usually three things at once: tax withheld, a valid claim, and a dispute. Most teams treat it as one. This case study frames that problem, maps the as-is process, and specifies a to-be workflow down to business rules, user stories and UAT.

1. Problem and scope

Signal (consolidated)FY25FY26Change
Revenue from operations₹2,136 Cr₹2,324 Cr+8.8%
Trade receivables₹658 Cr₹750 Cr+14.0%
Receivables above revenue pace (estimate)≈ ₹34 Cr

₹658 Cr grown at 8.8% ≈ ₹716 Cr, against ₹750 Cr actual. An estimate, not a measured backlog. Receivables can also rise through credit expansion, channel mix or year-end timing, so this justifies discovery, not a diagnosis.

Problem statement. When a distributor pays less than the invoice, nobody can see which part is tax, which part is a valid claim and which part is disputed. The gap waits in email until month-end, cash application is delayed, and the distributor keeps ordering on credit.

In scope

Distributor receipts, short payments and deductions; their classification, evidence, ownership, approval and settlement as credit notes.

Out of scope, and why

Credit-limit policy (only a flag is raised), collections strategy and ERP replacement. These are separate decisions with different owners.

        3. Business rules

        IDLayerRule
        BR-1IdentifyEach distributor pays into its own virtual account number, so every receipt is tagged to the payer. An unreferenced partial payment settles the oldest open invoice first.
        BR-2SplitTrack each distributor's running purchase total. TDS u/s 194Q applies when the buyer's previous-year turnover exceeds ₹10 crore and its purchases from the seller exceed ₹50 lakh in the year, at 0.1% of the amount above ₹50 lakh; once crossed, a TDS line is expected and matched to Form 26AS. GST-TDS (2%) applies only to notified buyers such as government bodies.
        BR-3ValidateAuto-accept a claim only if its evidence is present, it is within budget and it is under the Finance-set tolerance. Everything else goes to a person.
        BR-4ValidateUnexplained gaps are never written off by a rule.
        BR-5ControlAbove the approval threshold (illustratively ₹10,000) a second person approves; self-approval is blocked.
        BR-6PreventValid claims are settled as credit notes. Credit notes are reported to the IRP within 30 days (e-invoicing, turnover ₹10 Cr+, from 1 April 2025) and GST credit notes are issued no later than 30 November of the following year (Section 34).
        BR-7CreditWhen disputed deductions exceed a threshold or age past a limit, the next order is flagged for credit review.

        User stories and acceptance criteria (7)
        1. US-1 Identify payer. As an AR executive, I want each receipt tagged to its distributor, so I never identify payers from bank narrations. Given a receipt on a distributor's virtual account with no reference, when it is imported, then it is applied to that distributor's oldest open invoice and logged.
        2. US-2 Expect TDS. As an AR lead, I want TDS separated from deductions, so tax credits are not chased as losses. Given a distributor past the ₹50 lakh purchase threshold, when a receipt is short by the expected 0.1%, then the amount is classified as TDS and queued for the Form 26AS match.
        3. US-3 Auto-accept evidenced claims. As a Finance Controller, I want evidenced, in-budget claims under tolerance accepted automatically, so people work only exceptions. Given a scheme claim with its circular attached, within budget and under tolerance, when it is validated, then it is accepted and the rule that accepted it is recorded.
        4. US-4 Route disputes. As an AR lead, I want every unevidenced deduction to open a case with a reason code, owner and SLA, so nothing waits for month-end. Given a damage claim without proof of delivery, when it is classified, then a dispute opens, is assigned to the area sales manager and the SLA timer starts.
        5. US-5 Maker-checker. As a Finance Controller, I want deductions above the threshold approved by a second person, so write-offs are controlled. Given a case above ₹10,000, when its maker approves it, then the action is blocked, logged and routed to a checker.
        6. US-6 Settle upfront. As a Sales manager, I want valid scheme benefits issued as credit notes before payment, so the distributor pays in full. Given an approved claim, when the credit note is issued, then a 30-day IRP reporting deadline is set and alerts at day 20.
        7. US-7 Credit flag. As a credit controller, I want ageing disputes to flag the next order, so exposure doesn't compound. Given disputed deductions above the threshold or age limit, when the distributor places an order, then the order is flagged for review, not blocked.

        Also tested: UAT-01 to 03 and 08 to 10 are the positive cases of US-1 to 4, 6 and 7; UAT-04 to 07 are the negative control tests in the prototype above. Stories are written for a JIRA epic; Given/When/Then converts directly to test cases.

        5. Decisions and trade-offs

        DecisionWhat it costs
        Control before speed. Release 1 fixes ownership and approvals.Less automation at launch
        Strict matching at launch. Exact reference and amount only.Lower auto-match rate, in exchange for zero wrong matches
        ERP stays the system of record. A case layer (proposed: a workflow platform such as ServiceNow) posts only approved outcomes.Integration work, to confirm with IT
        No automatic write-off of unexplained gaps.Small items stay open longer and need a person
        Automate only the evidenced majority. BR-3 auto-accepts; people work exceptions.Needs a Finance-approved tolerance and clean evidence capture
        Prevent before you reconcile. Settle valid claims as credit notes.Sales must agree scheme budgets upfront

        6. Success metrics

        • Days deductions outstanding
        • Share of gaps auto-classified
        • Share of claims auto-accepted with evidence
        • Invalid deductions recovered
        • Credit notes reported within 30 days
        • Zero wrong write-offs

        Pilot on one division; targets set from the measured baseline, not assumed.

        7. Validation plan

        • Five 45-minute interviews: AR executive, AR lead, Finance Controller, area sales manager, ERP owner
        • One month of anonymised receipts
        • Stop rule: if differences are rare or resolved within days, stop and redirect to wherever the finance team spends the most manual effort.
