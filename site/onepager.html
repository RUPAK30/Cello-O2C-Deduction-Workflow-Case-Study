Business analysis case study · Order-to-cash · Rupak Ghanpathi

Every rupee needs an owner.

A distributor short payment is usually three things at once: tax withheld, a valid claim, and a dispute. I designed the workflow that separates them on the day the payment arrives.

The same ₹20,000 gap, two workflows (illustrative)

Today (hypothesised)

Day 31 · ₹20,000

still unexplained after 9 emails. Payer found by hand, owner unclear, approval over email, visible only at month-end.

Proposed workflow

09:03 · ₹0

unexplained on day 0. ₹1,000 TDS (a tax credit), ₹8,000 evidenced scheme claim accepted by rule, ₹11,000 damage claim disputed with an owner and a 7-day SLA.

Four layers

1 Identify. A virtual account per distributor tags every receipt to its payer; unreferenced payments settle the oldest invoice.

2 Split. TDS u/s 194Q is expected once purchases cross ₹50 lakh and matched to Form 26AS; it is a credit, not a loss.

3 Validate. Claims with evidence, within budget and tolerance are accepted by rule; everything else gets a reason, owner and SLA. Nothing is written off automatically.

4 Prevent. Valid claims become credit notes upfront, reported to the IRP within 30 days, so the next payment arrives in full.

Requirements delivered

  • 7 business rules (BR-1 to BR-7) and a reason-code evidence matrix
  • 7 user stories with Given/When/Then acceptance criteria
  • 10 UAT cases, including 4 negative control tests
  • Traceability from pain point and research to requirement and test
  • RACI across AR, Finance, Sales, IT and Audit

Validate and measure

  • Five interviews and one month of receipts; stop if gaps are rare or close fast
  • Days deductions outstanding · share auto-classified · invalid deductions recovered · credit notes reported in 30 days · zero wrong write-offs

Trade-offs

  • Control before speed; strict matching at launch; ERP stays the system of record; no automatic write-offs
